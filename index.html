About

Formation, then inference

I work at a point where two traditions rarely meet: the history of ideas — how people and institutions have formed their beliefs, characters, and loyalties over time — and the technology now built to model, predict, and shape belief and behavior in real time.

I trained as an economist before turning to intellectual history — a PhD from Cambridge, a master's from Yale, research fellowships at the Sorbonne and Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin. My scholarship has ranged across the history of philosophy, theology, and cultural sociology, including two books from Oxford University Press and a co-edited handbook on the history of theological reception. As a contributing editor at The Hedgehog Review, I write more often now on the questions that history keeps raising for the present: institutional decay and managerialism, the politics of memory, the state of higher education, and what AI is doing to belief, trust, and attention.

In 2022, I co-founded FilterLabs with David P. Smith, applying a version of the same question — how people come to trust what they believe — to the mechanics of data and AI. FilterLabs' Ubiquity platform gives analysts and decision-makers source-verified, hyperlocal signal in noisy information environments, from conflict zones to humanitarian response. I led the company as CEO through its first years and now serve as Chairman, working alongside CEO Erol Yayboke.

Today my research splits between Harvard, where I co-direct the AI and Flourishing Initiative at the Human Flourishing Program, and the Faculty of Theology at Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin, where I hold an Einstein Fellowship. Both, in their own way, keep asking the question I started with: what actually forms a person, and what happens when we try to engineer it.

Technology

FilterLabs

Founded in 2022 with David P. Smith. FilterLabs' flagship platform, Ubiquity, delivers source-verified, hyperlocal data intelligence for defense, government, and humanitarian organizations working in fragmented or contested information environments.

Ubiquity in the field

Used by partners including the Development Intelligence Lab to track fast-moving political and social dynamics — from Nepal to active conflict zones — where reliable, local-level signal is hardest to get and matters most.

Earlier: predictive tools at UVA

At the University of Virginia, led a team of data scientists and scholars building machine learning tools to anticipate political and social violence — the direct precursor to FilterLabs.
